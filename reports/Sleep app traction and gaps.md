# Kudoze wins with bedtime crews, not kudos

The founder is half right. Sleep is not social the way sport is, and a public "Strava for sleep" feed where friends give kudos to each other's scores is the weakest version of Kudoze: at least five indie apps already ship that model with no visible traction, Oura and Whoop have kept their social sleep features small and private, and the one mass-market sleep hit, Pokémon Sleep (20M+ downloads, about $235M gross), wins on a single-player collection game, not on friends. Sleep is social in a different place, though. Bedtimes are pushed late by friends, beds are shared by couples whose sleep is measurably synchronised, and the behaviours that actually improve sleep (a fixed wake time, a lights-out time, a caffeine cut-off) are controllable inputs that respond to commitment and small-group accountability. The evidence points to a narrower product: a solo morning recap that is useful with zero friends, plus small private "crews" (a partner, a student house, a running club) that commit to inputs together in the evening, built on Kudoze's one real structural advantage, device-agnostic wearable import. Keep the import, the friends-only visibility and the lights-out challenge; drop score leaderboards, deep-sleep competition and the open follow graph as the growth engine; build the solo loop and crews first; and get first users by hand-seeding small groups in Belgium the way Strava's founders recruited cycling clubs. Throughout this report, cited prose is sourced from the research notes; paragraphs marked **Inference** are reasoning from those sources, and anything marked **From the codebase** comes from reading the nightfeed repo, not from the research.

## Strava's engine runs on effort, and sleep has little of it

Strava's numbers are real and large: over 180 million users by December 2025 and about 50 million monthly actives in 2025 by Sensor Tower's estimate ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/12/strava-eyes-ipo-as-gen-z-trades-dating-apps-for-running-clubs/); [the5krunner](https://the5krunner.com/2026/01/09/strava-ipo-filing-3-billion-valuation-analysis/)). Users gave **more than 14 billion kudos** in its 2025 reporting year, activities with photos got **more than 3x the kudos**, and clubs nearly quadrupled to **1 million** ([Athletech News](https://athletechnews.com/running-walking-strength-training-strava-year-in-sport-report/); [Webull/PR Newswire](https://www.webull.com/news/13961203642123264)). The social effect on behaviour is causal and measured: in a study of about 1.1 million runners, **one extra km run by friends led to about 0.3 extra km** by the individual the same day, driven by upward comparison ([MIT Sloan](https://mitsloan.mit.edu/node/9025)). In the STEP UP trial, 3-person competitive leaderboards added about **920 steps a day** and were the only arm whose effect survived after the game ended ([PubMed 31498375](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31498375/)).

Every one of those mechanics assumes the activity is chosen, effortful, legible and better when there is more of it. Sleep outcomes fail that test. Sleep scores and stages are only partly under the user's control, and trying harder backfires: in a randomised trial, insomnia patients told (falsely) that they slept badly reported a much larger drop in alertness (**d = 0.79**) regardless of how they actually slept ([PubMed 29989248](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29989248/)). Orthosomnia, anxiety from chasing sleep metrics, affects an estimated **3% to 14%** of tracker users depending on the definition ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11592250/)), and a 2025 US survey found **30.9%** of trackers screened positive for orthosomnia *risk* on a broader screener ([SLEEP abstract](https://academic.oup.com/sleep/article/49/Supplement_1/A246/8674102)). The AASM itself tells tracker users to focus on duration, consistent bed and wake times and night awakenings rather than chasing scores ([AASM](https://aasm.org/sleep-tracking-and-sleepmaxxing-change-bedtime-behaviors-and-keep-some-americans-awake-at-night/)).

The data quality makes score comparison worse. Across six wrist devices tested against polysomnography in 2025, four-stage agreement was only **kappa 0.21 to 0.53**, wake specificity was **29% to 52%**, and five of six overestimated total sleep, by about **39 minutes (Withings), 38 (Garmin), 24 (Whoop) and 19 (Apple Watch)** ([Schyvens et al., PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12038347/)). Timing, by contrast, is measured well: regularity can be computed from sleep and wake times alone, and in 60,977 UK Biobank participants **sleep regularity predicted all-cause mortality better than duration** (20% to 48% lower mortality for the more regular quintiles; observational) ([Flinders](https://researchnow.flinders.edu.au/en/publications/sleep-regularity-is-a-stronger-predictor-of-mortality-risk-than-s/)). Apple's free Sleep Score already weights duration and bedtime consistency at **80 of 100 points** and gives stages nothing ([AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/articles/25/09/12/how-sleep-score-works-on-apple-watch-with-watchos-26)).

**Inference.** Kudos on a sleep score rewards something the user mostly did not choose, compares devices more than people, and feeds the exact anxiety that harms sleep. The founder's doubt is correct for the *outcome* layer. It is wrong for the *input* layer: lights-out time, wake time and consistency are chosen, legible, comparable across devices, and the same kind of narrow, controllable unit that Strava segments were.

**From the codebase.** Kudoze's server score currently weights stages at 20% (`src/data/score.ts`, `WEIGHTS`), and one of the three challenge types is a streak of nights above a minimum score (`server/state.ts`, `progressFor`). Both put the weakest, least controllable signal into the social layer. The `lights_out` challenge type and the `followers`/`only_me` visibility model are already aligned with the evidence.

## Five lookalikes and two giants show the feed is not the moat

The "Strava for sleep" slot is crowded and quiet. Slumber: Social Sleep has leaderboards, clubs and a friend "sleep compatibility" score from Apple Health and Health Connect ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/slumber-social-sleep/id6772449516)); RankedSleep ranks friends on a 0 to 100 score ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/ca/app/rankedsleep/id6755540163)); Downtime posts a HealthKit auto-score to a friends-only feed with reactions and comments, which is almost exactly Kudoze's core ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/downtime-share-your-sleep/id6758105212)); Sleeping With Friends runs weekly leaderboards on total, REM and deep sleep across Fitbit, Garmin, Whoop and Oura ([sleepingwithfriends.com](https://sleepingwithfriends.com/)); and Sleepover: Sleep to Compete is on the App Store too ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1556384666)). None shows funding or traction in the research, which is absence of evidence rather than proof of failure.

The incumbents chose small and private. Oura Circles (2023) shares two weeks of high-level scores with emoji reactions, capped at 10 circles per member and 20 members per circle (TechCrunch reported 10), Oura-only ([Oura support](https://support.ouraring.com/hc/en-us/articles/15958088640147); [Android Authority](https://androidauthority.com/oura-circles-3332965)). Oura framed it around loneliness, and its showcase story is a married couple ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2023/06/08/oura-launches-new-social-sleep-features-for-health-tracker-market.html); [Oura blog](https://ouraring.com/blog/ar/how-kati-and-jon-use-oura-circles/)). Whoop Teams puts sleep on leaderboards inside existing sports and workplace teams, and in May 2026 Whoop was reportedly testing an individual "follow friends" feature with employees only ([Whoop Locker](https://whoop.com/thelocker/join-create-teams-on-whoop); [Gadgets & Wearables](https://gadgetsandwearables.com/2026/05/06/whoop-follow-friends/)). Whoop's 31-day sleep challenge had **38,838 finishers**, small next to its 2.5M+ members ([Pulse](https://www.pulse.bot/motivation/news/nearly-39000-complete-31day-sleep-challenge-show-measurable-gains-in-rest-eb60042b-7edd-4090-88b3-89ef16bfc433/); [Sacra](https://sacra.com/research/whoop)). The oldest academic attempt, Somnometer (2013), found that sharing sleep raised connectedness among friends, but users were **wary of the broadcast nature** of social networks ([Somnometer paper](https://www.eti.uni-siegen.de/ubicomp/papers/ess_ijhci2013.pdf)).

Meanwhile the basic score is a commodity. Apple gives one away free on watchOS 26, Samsung bundles sleep features free with its watches, and Oura, Fitbit and Garmin charge around $70 a year for interpretation and AI coaching ([AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/articles/25/09/12/how-sleep-score-works-on-apple-watch-with-watchos-26); [Kracked](https://www.kracked.com/smart-ring-subscription-cost/); [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/health-fitness/garmin-connect-plus)). Every big player's 2025 to 2026 move is an LLM coach (Oura Advisor, Fitbit's Gemini coach, Garmin Active Intelligence, Eight Sleep's Sleep Agent) ([Google blog](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/devices/fitbit/personal-health-coach-public-preview/); [Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250819119667/en/Eight-Sleep-Secures-%2524100-Million-to-Accelerate-AIPowered-Sleep-Optimization-Expand-Into-Medical-and-Grow-Global-Footprint)), and TechRadar found Garmin's AI sleep summaries "don't really say much of anything" ([TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/health-fitness/smartwatches/garmins-new-subscription-ai-feature-is-hilariously-bad-so-far)). App-only tracking is shrinking: Sleep Cycle, the listed pure play, fell from **918k paying subscribers at end 2024 to 665k in Q2 2026** while staying profitable, and its fastest-growing line is partnerships (+46% in Q4 2025, 12% of sales) ([Sleep Cycle year-end 2025](https://storage.mfn.se/79dd61f8-4ccc-4bc2-b095-feedaf3fced8/sleep-cycle-year-end-report-2025.pdf); [Q2 2026 report](https://storage.mfn.se/c8ce169d-d4d5-47a3-b5f5-29157c80424e/sleep-cycle-interim-report-apr-jun-2026.pdf)).

**Inference.** Kudoze cannot win on a better score, a smarter AI coach or a feed: the first two belong to companies with hardware and billions in funding, and the third is already cloned. The structural opening is the one incumbents cannot fill: every hardware maker locks its social layer to its own device (Circles is Oura-only, Teams is Whoop-only, Apple's score is Apple Watch-only), so a couple with an Oura and an Apple Watch, or a running club with five brands, has nowhere to go ([Oura support](https://support.ouraring.com/hc/en-us/articles/15958088640147); [Whoop Locker](https://www.whoop.com/thelocker/join-create-teams-on-whoop/)). Open Wearables import is Kudoze's moat; the feed is a feature. That moat also carries a cost: cross-brand comparison of total sleep or stages is unfair by up to 20 minutes per night, so the social layer must compare people to their own baseline or on timing.

## Sleep turns social at bedtime, in pairs and small groups

The research finds three places where sleep is genuinely social. First, friends cause late bedtimes: in college students, bedtime was strongly correlated with the timing of the last objectively measured social interaction with friends, and "social bedtime procrastination" was linked to the need to belong ([Respiratory Therapy](https://respiratory-therapy.com/disorders-diseases/sleep-medicine/insomnias/how-late-college-students-go-to-sleep-influenced-by-need-to-belong/)). Second, couples are biologically coupled: polysomnography of co-sleeping couples showed sleep architecture synchronised within about plus or minus five minutes, with synchronisation linked to relationship depth ([Frontiers in Psychiatry](https://www.frontiersin.org/article/10.3389/fpsyt.2020.00583/full)), and an actigraphy study found same-bed couples awake or asleep together about 75% of the time (via summary, not verified) ([Sleep Review](https://sleepreviewmag.com/?p=16203)). Third, commitment works on inputs: paying low-income workers per extra minute of sleep added about **33 minutes**, and encouragement alone about 20, entirely through more time in bed ([J-PAL](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/evaluation/economic-and-behavioral-effects-increasing-sleep-among-low-income-workers-india)); incentivised US students were still about **9% more likely** to sleep 7 to 9 hours six weeks after payments stopped, and showed demand for commitment devices (figures from summary, PDF not verified) ([NBER w30375](https://nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w30375/w30375.pdf)); and group sessions with reminders gave adolescents earlier weekday bedtimes sustained for six months ([Psychiatric News](https://alert.psychnews.org/2025/01/motivational-intervention-may-help.html)). Small-group sharing has older support too: BuddyClock found that sharing sleep status with a few friends changed behaviour and increased connectedness ([ACM via Unpaywall](https://unpaywall.org/10.1145%2F1460563.1460660)). No trial has tested a social feed, kudos or friend leaderboards on sleep outcomes specifically.

The science also says which inputs deserve to be the shared goal. Multicomponent CBT-I is the only strongly recommended insomnia treatment, and sleep hygiene alone is recommended against ([NeurologyLive](https://www.neurologylive.com/view/new-aasm-guidelines-make-strong-recommendation-for-behavioral-therapy-in-chronic-insomnia)); a network meta-analysis of **241 trials** found cognitive restructuring, sleep restriction and stimulus control do the work ([BORIS Bern](https://boris.unibe.ch/191753)). Weekend catch-up sleep did not undo weekday restriction in a lab trial ([NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/health/mental-health/catch-sleep-weekend-may-increase-waistline-study-finds-n977916)). Caffeine has a numeric rule: a regular coffee at least **8.8 hours** before bed to avoid losing sleep ([Leeds Beckett](https://eprints.leedsbeckett.ac.uk/id/eprint/9625/1/TheEffectOfCaffeineOnSubsequentSleepASystematicReviewAndMetaanalysisPV-WEAKLEY.pdf)). More daytime light and less night light are associated with better mental health in 86,772 people ([Nature Mental Health](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44220-023-00135-8.pdf)).

**Inference.** The right social object for Kudoze is not "my night" in the morning but "our evening" and "our wake time". Ranked by fit with the evidence: a couple or household mode (the smallest network, interdependent by nature, useful from the first invite); private crews of three to eight friends who commit to the same lights-out or wake time and see only whether each kept it; an accountability buddy for a pledge; and a synchronous "lights out" moment that flips peer influence from keeping people up to sending them to bed (untested). Each of these makes the second user valuable to the first, unlike a follow. Public broadcast sits at the bottom, with the highest orthosomnia and privacy risk and a role better played by TikTok, where #sleepmaxxing passed 500 million views (low-reliability source) ([Fawcett Mattress](https://fawcettmattress.com/blogs/news/sleepmaxxing-statistics)).

## The open gaps are actionability, insomnia support and the shared bed

Users already pay around $70 a year per wearable and revolt against more fees: Garmin's Connect+ launch drew a 6,000+ upvote boycott post ([TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/health-fitness/smartwatches/furious-garmin-users-revolt-over-new-subscription-service-we-need-to-take-a-firm-stand)). Their main complaint is data without direction: Consumer Reports' tester concluded the best tracker is one you can ignore ([Consumer Reports](https://www.consumerreports.org/sleeping/sleep-trackers-for-better-sleep-what-i-learned-a7533450811/)). Tracker abandonment studies blame low perceived usefulness, boredom and lost motivation, and find most abandonment is not permanent ([arXiv 1904.13226](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.13226); [the5krunner summary](https://the5krunner.com/2022/10/13/sport-tracker-watches-reasons-abandonment/)). Methods researchers warn that tracker "insights" are correlations, and propose structured within-person experiments instead ([arXiv 2208.00739](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.00739v4)).

Insomnia is the largest, best-evidenced unmet need: at least about 10% of adults ([Frontiers in Sleep](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sleep/articles/10.3389/frsle.2023.1279903/pdf)), an effective treatment, and little access. Fully automated digital CBT-I works in RCTs, but benefit scales with adherence and **over 40% drop out** ([DOAJ, npj Digital Medicine 2025](https://doaj.org/article/dbfe6bf435d7445faee348235881cbea); [Elsevier](https://www.elsevier.es/en-revista-international-journal-clinical-health-psychology-355-articulo-exploring-dropout-in-internet-delivered-cognitive-S1697260025000560)); only 58% of Sleepio's trial participants completed four or more weeks ([EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/570746)). The reimbursed route is closed for a small Belgian team: Belgium's mHealth pyramid reimburses at Level 3 mostly for heart failure and oncology monitoring, with no sleep pathway ([MTRC](https://mtrconsult.com/node/2863)); Germany's DiGA pays for insomnia apps such as somnio but needs medical-device status and evidence ([KVB](https://kvb.de/fileadmin/kvb/Mitglieder/Verordnungen/VO-aktuell/2025-DS/KVB-VA-250314-SOP-Digitale-Gesundheitsanwendungen-Psychotherapeuten.pdf)); and Pear Therapeutics, maker of Somryst, went bankrupt in 2023 and sold its assets for about $6M ([Fierce Biotech](https://www.fiercebiotech.com/medtech/pear-pulped-digital-therapeutics-makers-assets-sold-6m-auction-after-bankruptcy-filing)).

Two further groups are badly served. Shift workers: Sleep Research Society guidance says evidence does not support consumer wearables for daytime sleep ([Dovepress](https://www.dovepress.com/daytime-sleep-tracking-performance-of-four-commercial-wearable-devices-peer-reviewed-fulltext-article-NSS)), and scores that reward bedtime consistency structurally penalise rotating rosters. Couples: no affordable product treats the shared bed as one unit; Eight Sleep's dual-zone bed costs $2,600+ plus a subscription ([the5krunner](https://the5krunner.com/2025/05/14/eight-sleep-pod-5-everything-you-need-to-know/)), and Oura has no family plan, with Circles limited to Oura owners ([Kracked](https://www.kracked.com/smart-ring-subscription-cost/)). EU users also get less: Fitbit's Gemini coach and SleepioRx launched US-only ([9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2025/10/27/fitbit-coach-preview/); [Big Health](https://www.bighealth.com/news/us-fda-grants-clearance-for-sleepiorx)).

**Inference.** Three features are worth building because they are missing and fit Kudoze's strengths. A *together experiment*: a crew picks one evidence-backed lever (fixed wake time, caffeine cut-off, morning light) for two weeks, and each member sees their own before/after on robust metrics (wake time, duration against their own baseline, resting heart rate), which turns data into action and attacks the motivation decay behind abandonment. A *personal social-jetlag number* (weekday versus weekend wake-time gap), cheap to compute from imported nights and rarely surfaced as a headline, framed as "shrink the gap", not "never sleep in" ([Nature Reviews Endocrinology](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41574-023-00851-2.pdf)). And a *CBT-I-informed buddy mode* later: the aversive early weeks of a fixed sleep window are where people quit, and a partner who sees whether you got up on time targets that friction, without any treatment claim that would pull Kudoze under medical-device rules ([AFMPS](https://www.afmps.be/en/human_use/health_products/medical_devices_and_their_accessories/manufacturers/health_applications)). A shift-worker mode is a real gap but a later, possibly B2B, bet.

## Prioritised recommendation

Indie health apps face hard arithmetic. Typical cross-category retention is about **26% on day 1, 13% on day 7 and 7% on day 30** ([Adjust](https://www.adjust.com/blog/what-makes-a-good-retention-rate/)); only about **5% of Health and Fitness apps reach $10k revenue within two years**, and median revenue per install at day 60 is about **$0.63** ([Athletech News](https://athletechnews.com/fitness-apps-monetizable-winner-take-all-or-most/)). Social networks have no value until friends join, so the standard fix is "come for the tool, stay for the network" ([cdixon.org](https://cdixon.org/2015/01/31/come-for-the-tool-stay-for-the-network)); BeReal shows a friends-only daily ritual can lose **61% of daily users** in five months once novelty fades ([Stealth Optional](https://stealthoptional.com/news/bereal-bought-by-mobile-games-publisher-despite-audience-decline)). The growth stories that fit a team without IP or ad budget are Strava, whose founders started with a leaderboard on a few cycling segments and recruited local clubs by hand ([Alexander Jarvis](https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/strava-doing-things-that-dont-scale); [social.plus](https://www.social.plus/blog/how-strava-formed-the-worlds-biggest-team-a-community-case-study)), and Finch, which grew almost entirely through unprompted user TikToks of its distinctive pet bird ([GrowthMap](https://growthmap-www.pages.dev/blog/finch-growth-teardown/)). Duolingo's streak wager lifted day-7 retention by up to 14% ([Duolingo blog](https://making.duolingo.com/how-streaks-keep-duolingo-learners-committed-to-their-language-goals)).

The table below is the recommendation. The "Basis" column says whether each line rests on sourced evidence (cited above) or on inference.

| Priority | Decision | What it means for Kudoze | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Keep | Device-agnostic wearable import (Open Wearables, Apple Health, Health Connect) | The one thing incumbents structurally cannot offer; make "your crew can use any device" the pitch | Sourced: device lock-in of Circles, Teams, Apple score. Inference: that it is the moat |
| Keep | Friends-only visibility, no public profile | Matches Somnometer's broadcast wariness and every incumbent's choice | Sourced |
| Keep | `lights_out` challenge, comments, reactions, Plume, photo of the day | Inputs are controllable; photos triple kudos on Strava; a mascot drove Finch | Sourced mechanics, inference on transfer to sleep |
| Change | Score: drop stages from the headline, lead with bedtime, wake time and consistency; add a no-numbers mode | Stage kappa 0.21 to 0.53; negative feedback worsens next-day alertness | Sourced |
| Change | Kudos: reward kept commitments ("in bed by 23:30", "up at 7:00"), not a good score | Effort and choice are what kudos mean on Strava | Inference |
| Drop | Score-streak challenges, deep/REM or raw-duration leaderboards across devices | Rewards hardware and noise, feeds orthosomnia | Sourced (device bias, orthosomnia); inference on product effect |
| Drop | Open follow graph and "global feed" as the growth plan; any insomnia treatment claim; paid ads | Feed is cloned and unproven; claims trigger MDR; ads do not pay back at $0.63 per install | Sourced facts, inference on the decision |
| Build 1 | Solo morning recap that works with zero friends: last night's timing, consistency against your own 14-night baseline, social-jetlag gap, Plume's reaction | Solves cold start; daily wake-up is a natural trigger | Sourced (Dixon, regularity science); inference on design |
| Build 2 | Crews of 2 to 8, with couple/household mode as the default first invite: shared lights-out or wake-time target, crew streak where everyone must keep it, evening "lights out" moment | The smallest atomic network; bedtime is socially caused | Sourced (couples sync, social bedtime procrastination, commitment trials); synchronous mechanic untested |
| Build 3 | Two-week together experiments (wake time, caffeine cut-off at about 9 h, morning light) with personal before/after | Fixes "data without direction" | Sourced levers; inference on format |
| Build 4 | Shareable weekly card and year-end recap, story-sized, no raw health data | Finch/Strava-style organic distribution | Inference |
| Later | CBT-I-informed buddy programme (wellness wording), shift-worker mode, club or workplace team challenges, partnerships | Highest-need gaps and Calm/Sleep Cycle's growth lines, but slower or riskier | Sourced gaps; inference on timing |

For first users, the Strava playbook translates directly. Convert the waitlist by asking each person to start a crew of two or three, not to create a profile. Hand-seed atomic networks in Belgium where schedules already align and wearables are common: student houses in Leuven and Ghent (the students who sleep late because of each other), running and cycling clubs whose members already own Garmins and Whoops and already understand kudos, and couples, pitched as "one app for both of your devices". Run one seasonal, input-based challenge at a time (for example a January "same wake time for 21 days" crew challenge, echoing Pokémon Sleep's best month being a December event ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/pokmon-sleep-surpasses-230m-in-three-years/))). Keep the core free; if a subscription comes, Sleep Cycle's ARPU of about SEK 277 a year (roughly €24, own conversion) is the realistic ceiling for an app without hardware ([Sleep Cycle year-end 2025](https://storage.mfn.se/79dd61f8-4ccc-4bc2-b095-feedaf3fced8/sleep-cycle-year-end-report-2025.pdf)).

**Inference, test before scaling.** Treat the first eight weeks after opening as an experiment with three signals: the share of new users who are in a crew of two or more within seven days, day-30 retention of crew members against solo users and against the roughly 7% benchmark, and the share of crews still active with all members after four weeks. If crew members do not retain clearly better than solo users, the social thesis is falsified for Kudoze, and the product should fall back to the solo morning recap and experiments, which stand on their own.

## Conclusion

The founder framed the question as "sport is social, sleep isn't", but the research reframes it: sport is social at the finish line, sleep is social at the start line. What people do together is go to bed, wake up, share a bed and keep each other up; what they cannot do together without harm is compare outcomes their bodies and their devices produce. That moves Kudoze from "Strava for sleep" toward something closer to Strava's original, smaller idea, a narrow controllable segment shared within a club, applied to bedtime and wake time instead of a climb.

The biggest unknown remains untested: no trial has shown that a social layer improves sleep or retention in a sleep app, and the lookalikes' silence is weak evidence either way. Kudoze's advantage is that it can find out cheaply. Its device-agnostic import is already built, its visibility model is already private, and the changes recommended here are mostly subtractions from the score and challenge logic plus one new object, the crew. If crews retain, Kudoze has something no hardware maker can copy without opening its walls; if they do not, the solo loop is still a better product than another score.
