# Social mechanics for sleep vs. sport ("Strava for sleep")

Research date: 2026-11-16. Notes compiled from web search and fetch. Most findings come from search-result summaries of press coverage; items marked "not verified" were not checked against the primary document. Older data is dated inline.

## 1. Why does Strava work (effort/achievement signalling, segments, clubs, kudos, identity)?

### Takeaway
Strava works because exercise is a visible, effortful, voluntary achievement that people do together and at shared times. Kudos, photos, clubs and group activities create a feedback loop, and peer-reviewed data shows that friends' posted runs measurably change one's own running. The core inputs are effort and choice, which sleep mostly lacks.

### Cited Findings
- Strava reported over 180 million users as of December 2025, up from more than 150 million in August 2024. Sensor Tower estimates about 50 million monthly active users in 2025, with downloads up 80% year on year — [TechCrunch, Oct 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/12/strava-eyes-ipo-as-gen-z-trades-dating-apps-for-running-clubs/); [the5krunner, Jan 2026](https://the5krunner.com/2026/01/09/strava-ipo-filing-3-billion-valuation-analysis/)
- Strava raised at a $2.2B valuation in May 2025 (led by Sequoia). It reportedly filed confidentially for an IPO in January 2026. Revenue figures of about $430M–$490M for 2025 are third-party estimates (Sacra and others), not official — [Gadgets & Wearables, Jan 2026](https://gadgetsandwearables.com/2026/01/09/strava-ipo/); [Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/strava/)
- Year in Sport 2025 (data 1 Sep 2024 – 30 Aug 2025, survey of 30,494 respondents): users gave more than 14 billion kudos, about 20% more than the year before. Activities with photos got more than 3x the kudos of activities without photos — [Athletech News](https://athletechnews.com/running-walking-strength-training-strava-year-in-sport-report/); [BikeRadar](https://www.bikeradar.com/news/strava-year-in-sport-2025)
- Year in Sport 2025: new clubs nearly quadrupled, to 1 million clubs in total. 39% of Gen Z say they exercise to meet people with shared interests. The report frames this as "doomscrolling is out, movement is in" — [Webull/PR Newswire](https://www.webull.com/news/13961203642123264); [Running Insight](https://www.runninginsight.com/movement-is-in-running-is-cool-and-community-rules)
- Coverage of Strava's IPO plans ties its growth to Gen Z "trading dating apps for running clubs": the social value is in-person and synchronous (group runs), not only the feed — [TechCrunch, Oct 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/12/strava-eyes-ipo-as-gen-z-trades-dating-apps-for-running-clubs/)
- Aral & Nicolaides (Nature Communications, 2017), "Exercise contagion in a global social network": a quasi-experiment on about 1.1M runners whose tracker runs were auto-posted to friends found that one extra km run by friends was associated with about 0.3 extra km by the individual on the same day. Less active runners influenced more active runners, but not the reverse (upward comparison/competitive drive). The effect was strongest man-to-man — [MIT Sloan](https://mitsloan.mit.edu/node/9025); [DOAJ abstract](https://doaj.org/article/38f276131ff14900890cce5839bde403)
- STEP UP RCT (Patel et al., JAMA Internal Medicine 2019; 602 adults, 24-week intervention plus 12-week follow-up): competition (3-person leaderboards) added about 920 steps a day versus control, support about 689 and collaboration about 637. Only competition kept a significant effect after the game ended — [PubMed 31498375](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31498375/); [EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/901675)

### Inferences
- Strava's mechanics share one precondition: the activity is effortful, chosen, legible (distance, pace, elevation, route photo) and often done together. Kudos reward visible effort, segments reward comparable performance on the same terrain, and clubs and group runs reward co-presence.
- The contagion result rests on upward comparison ("they ran, so I'll run"). It works because more running is better and is under the user's control on the same day.
- Competition beating support and collaboration in STEP UP suggests leaderboards drive behaviour when the metric is controllable. That is the weak spot for sleep (see section 4).

### Gaps
- No primary Strava document on retention lift from kudos or clubs was found (for example, "club members are X% more active"). Strava's own Year in Sport PDF was not retrieved directly.
- Strava's official subscriber count is not public.

## 2. Which social sleep products have been tried, and how did they do?

### Takeaway
The commercially successful "social-ish" sleep product is Pokémon Sleep, and it succeeds through collection and gamification, not through a friend feed. Wearable makers (Oura Circles, WHOOP Teams) have added small private groups or team leaderboards, but these are side features. Pure "friends see your sleep" apps have stayed niche or academic. No clear funded "Strava for sleep" startup success was found.

### Cited Findings
- **Pokémon Sleep** (launched July 2023): passed 20 million downloads by its first anniversary in July 2024 — [Pokémon press release](https://press.pokemon.com/en/Pokemon-Sleep-Celebrates-One-Year-Anniversary-and-Twenty-Million-Downl)
- Pokémon Sleep revenue: about $100M in year one (AppMagic estimate) — [Niche Gamer](https://nichegamer.com/pokemon-sleep-celebrates-first-anniversary). About $150M gross after about 1.5 years, with Japan the largest market at $109M (AppMagic) — [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/pokmon-sleep-slumbers-over-150m-mark-despite-slowdown-since-first-anniversary). About $234.9M lifetime by its third anniversary in July 2026; best month of year three was December 2025 at almost $6.7M; revenue declining year on year but the rate of decline is slowing — [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/pokmon-sleep-surpasses-230m-in-three-years/); [Outlook Respawn](https://respawn.outlookindia.com/gaming/gaming-news/pok%C3%A9mon-sleep-hits-2349m-revenue-on-3rd-anniversary)
- Appfigures (Oct 2025) uses a different method (net of store fees): about $112M net since launch by about Sep 2025. Over the same two months Sleep Cycle earned about $37M, about $1.1M a month net (down from about $1.3M a month before Pokémon Sleep launched). Appfigures states: "Pokémon Sleep isn't competing over Sleep Cycle's audience for the most part." — [Appfigures](https://appfigures.com/resources/insights/20251003/amp?f=1). Note: gross (AppMagic) and net (Appfigures) figures differ and should not be mixed.
- Sensor Tower Q2 2025, US: Pokémon Sleep weekly downloads fell from about 15K to about 8K (unified); US iOS weekly revenue was $42K–$126K. Pokémon Sleep is about 3.7% of Pokémon mobile IAP in 2026, far behind GO and TCG Pocket — [Sensor Tower Q2 2025](https://sensortower.com/blog/2025-q2-unified-top-5-pokemon%20collector%20games-revenue-us-6089902328293296048c7c1b); [mobilegamer.biz](https://mobilegamer.biz/data-digest-q1s-top-performers-milestones-for-pokemon-tcg-pocket-heartopia-efootball-and-plenty-more/)
- **Oura Circles** (launched June 2023): private groups sharing high-level Sleep, Readiness and Activity scores from the past two weeks, Oura members only, with emoji reactions. Up to 10 circles per user. Members per circle: 20 per Oura support and Android Authority, 10 per TechCrunch (conflict). Oura framed it as a response to loneliness — [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2023/06/08/oura-launches-new-social-sleep-features-for-health-tracker-market.html); [Wareable](https://www.wareable.com/wearable-tech/oura-circles-launches-share-sleep); [Android Authority](https://androidauthority.com/oura-circles-3332965)
- Oura's own Circles story is about a couple ("Kati, 42, and Jon, 45") using it together, which points to the couple and close-tie use case — [Oura blog](https://ouraring.com/blog/ar/how-kati-and-jon-use-oura-circles/)
- Press pushback on Oura Circles: "Sorry, Oura, I'm not ready to turn sleep into a competition just yet" — [TechRadar](https://techradar.com/opinion/sorry-oura-im-not-ready-to-turn-sleep-into-a-competition-just-yet)
- **WHOOP Teams**: leaderboards on strain, recovery and sleep. The team owner fixes which metrics are shared, and a user can join up to 10 teams. A May 2026 report says WHOOP is testing an individual "follow friends" feature, employees only and not announced — [WHOOP Locker](https://whoop.com/thelocker/join-create-teams-on-whoop); [Gadgets & Wearables, May 2026](https://gadgetsandwearables.com/2026/05/06/whoop-follow-friends/)
- **SleepTown** (Seekrtech, makers of Forest, which has more than 25M users): a gamified bedtime and wake-time goal app (you build a town if you keep the schedule). It has "circles" with family and friends for shared sleep goals. No reliable download figure was found — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/sg/app/sleeptown/id1210251567?uo=2); [Apptopia](https://apptopia.com/ios/app/1210251567/about)
- Indie "post your night" apps exist: Slumber (Product Hunt; reads Apple Health, posts your night to friends, Compare tab) and "Sleepover: Sleep to Compete" (App Store). No traction data was found — [Product Hunt Slumber](https://www.producthunt.com/products/slumber-3); [App Store Sleepover](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1556384666)
- Sleep Keeker (older alarm app) let you see when friends wake up. No sign of traction or current status — [PhoneArena](https://www.phonearena.com/news/Sleep-Keeker-alarm-clock-app-lets-you-see-what-time-your-friends-wake-up_id64235)
- Failed sleep hardware startups (not social): Hello (Sense) shut down in 2017 and Zeo closed in 2013 — [MobiHealthNews](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/sleep-tracking-company-hello-silicon-valley-darling-has-shut-down)

### Inferences
- The one sleep product with mass traction (Pokémon Sleep: 20M+ downloads, more than $200M gross) wins on collection, IP and a morning reward ritual. Its loop is single-player. That suggests gamification and reward beat social feed for sleep.
- Incumbents with large sleep-data user bases (Oura, WHOOP) chose small, private, opt-in groups or teams rather than a public feed. Oura framed it as connection ("loneliness"), not competition. WHOOP's leaderboards sit inside a fitness-first community.
- Sleep apps that put friends at the centre (Slumber, Sleepover, Sleep Keeker, SleepTown circles) show no public evidence of scale. Absence of evidence, not proof of failure.

### Gaps
- No usage or adoption numbers for Oura Circles or WHOOP Teams were found (what share of members use them, and whether they lift retention).
- Fitbit sleep challenges, Rise Science and Sleep Reset social features were not researched due to tool-call limits.
- No "BeReal for sleep" app with reported traction was found. No documented post-mortem of a funded social-sleep startup was found.
- No verified download data for SleepTown.

## 3. Evidence on social accountability, buddies, group challenges and financial commitment for sleep behaviour change

### Takeaway
Money and structured group programmes can move sleep duration and bedtime modestly (about 20–35 minutes, or earlier weekday bedtimes), mainly by adding time in bed. Evidence for peer or social-feed accountability specifically for sleep is thin and mostly small or feasibility-level. The strongest social-competition evidence (STEP UP) is for steps, not sleep.

### Cited Findings
- Bessone, Rao, Schilbach, Schofield, Toma (QJE 2021), Chennai, India, 452 low-income adults: incentives of INR 1 per extra minute of sleep (capped at 2 hours a day) raised sleep by about 33 minutes; encouragement alone by about 20 minutes. Gains came from more time in bed, not better sleep efficiency. Extra night sleep had no effect on productivity, earnings, decision-making or well-being; daytime naps did help — [J-PAL](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/evaluation/economic-and-behavioral-effects-increasing-sleep-among-low-income-workers-india); [NBER w26746](https://www.nber.org/papers/w26746.pdf)
- Avery, Giuntella, Jiao (NBER, US college students, wearable-tracked field experiment): monetary incentives raised sleep duration, with some persistence. Students who got incentives were still about 9% more likely to sleep 7–9 hours a night up to six weeks after incentives stopped. The authors report demand for commitment devices — [NBER w30375](https://nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w30375/w30375.pdf); [WFAE coverage](https://www.wfae.org/education/2022-09-03/one-way-to-help-college-students-get-enough-sleep-pay-them-to-go-to-bed) (figures from search summary; the PDF could not be read, not verified)
- Adolescent RCT with group sessions, motivational interviewing and text reminders: the intervention group had earlier weekday bedtimes after the intervention, sustained up to 6-month follow-up (diary-measured) — [Psychiatric News alert, Jan 2025](https://alert.psychnews.org/2025/01/motivational-intervention-may-help.html)
- School-based cluster RCT drawing on peer, caregiver and teacher influence: larger gains in sleep beliefs and self-efficacy, daytime sleepiness and internalising symptoms versus control — [Univ. of Iowa repository](https://iro.uiowa.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/A-Cluster-Randomised-Trial-of-a/9984832083302771)
- Small feasibility study (college students with bedtime procrastination), one group meeting plus home practice, using social motivation and commitment: improvements in bedtime procrastination, sleep efficiency and daytime fatigue — [medRxiv 2023](https://medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.04.16.23288655v1.full-text)
- Social context drives late bedtimes: a 2025 SLEEP meeting abstract links "social bedtime procrastination" to need to belong in college students. Bedtime was strongly correlated with the timing of the last objectively measured social interaction with friends — [Respiratory Therapy](https://respiratory-therapy.com/disorders-diseases/sleep-medicine/insomnias/how-late-college-students-go-to-sleep-influenced-by-need-to-belong/)
- STEP UP (steps, not sleep): competition beat support (a "sponsor" who gets weekly updates) and collaboration, and was the only arm that persisted after the game — [PubMed 31498375](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31498375/)

### Inferences
- The controllable sleep behaviours are bedtime, screen-off time, time in bed and wake time, not sleep score or deep sleep. Incentive and group trials moved exactly those inputs. A social product should target inputs (lights-out time, consistency), not outputs.
- Friends are often the cause of late bedtimes (last social interaction predicts bedtime). A group "lights out together" or "phone down" moment could flip peer influence from keeping people up to sending them to bed. This is a hypothesis, not a tested result.
- Demand for commitment (Avery et al.) suggests stakes, pledges or a buddy who sees whether you kept your bedtime could fit sleep better than kudos for a score.

### Gaps
- No RCT was found that tests a social feed, kudos or friend leaderboards on sleep outcomes specifically.
- Effect sizes for the adolescent group trial and the school trial come from summaries; full papers not reviewed.

## 4. Risks: orthosomnia, comparison anxiety, privacy, people sleeping at different times

### Takeaway
Sleep is a domain where trying harder backfires: anxiety about metrics is itself a sleep disruptor, and sleep scores are only partly under the user's control. Comparison and competition, which drive Strava, carry real risk here. Users are also wary of broadcasting sleep data.

### Cited Findings
- AASM 2025 Sleep Prioritization Survey: 48% of US adults used a sleep tracker in the past year (up from 35% in 2023); 55% adjust their sleep behaviour based on what they learn. The AASM reports that anxiety over optimal sleep metrics (orthosomnia) "keeps 76% of Americans awake at night" — [Managed Healthcare Executive, Jan 2026](https://www.managedhealthcareexecutive.com/view/don-t-let-sleepmaxxing-keep-you-up-at-night-results-from-the-2025-american-academy-of-sleep-medicine-survey-on-sleep-tracking); [AASM newsroom](https://aasm.org/sleep-tracking-and-sleepmaxxing-change-bedtime-behaviors-and-keep-some-americans-awake-at-night/). Caution: the 76% figure looks implausibly high as stated and likely refers to a subset (for example, tracker users who report some anxiety). The AASM page returned 403, so the exact wording and base could not be checked.
- The AASM advises tracker users to focus on duration, consistent bed and wake times, and night awakenings, rather than chasing scores — [AASM](https://aasm.org/sleep-tracking-and-sleepmaxxing-change-bedtime-behaviors-and-keep-some-americans-awake-at-night/)
- Clinicians quoted in sleepmaxxing coverage warn that over-monitoring makes people fixate on scores, causing anxiety that makes sleep harder — [ConsumerAffairs, Mar 2026](https://www.consumeraffairs.com/news/tracking-your-sleep-might-actually-be-keeping-you-up-at-night-032026.html); [Sleepopolis](https://sleepopolis.com/?p=188611)
- Oura users report stress after low scores, and press has raised privacy concerns about the ring — [ynetnews](https://www.ynetnews.com/health_science/article/hysw11ciwme)
- Somnometer (social alarm clock, IJHCS 2013; 725 downloads, 173 active users over 6 weeks): sharing sleep raised awareness and connectedness among friends, but users "were wary of the broadcast nature of current networks" and did not want to share sleep patterns indiscriminately — [Somnometer paper (Siegen)](https://www.eti.uni-siegen.de/ubicomp/papers/ess_ijhci2013.pdf)
- Sleep-score competition criticised in press as the wrong frame — [TechRadar](https://techradar.com/opinion/sorry-oura-im-not-ready-to-turn-sleep-into-a-competition-just-yet)

### Inferences
- Upward comparison, the engine of exercise contagion (section 1), is the mechanism most likely to cause orthosomnia in sleep. Leaderboards on sleep score are high-risk; leaderboards on inputs (in bed by target time, consistency streaks) are lower-risk but still need care.
- Sleep timing reveals presence and absence (when someone is home, asleep or awake, with whom). Public-by-default feeds carry privacy and safety risk beyond fitness, where Strava itself has had location privacy issues (not researched here).
- Different chronotypes, shift workers, parents and time zones mean there is no shared "race day". Synchronous mechanics only work in groups with similar schedules (households, couples, roommates, some teams).

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study was found that measures comparison anxiety caused by social sleep-score sharing specifically.
- AASM 76% figure: base population not verified.
- Sleep-data privacy incidents and regulation (for example GDPR special-category health data) not researched.

## 5. Alternative social formats that fit sleep better

### Takeaway
The evidence favours close, co-located or small groups (bed partners, households, close friends) and input-focused commitment (bedtime pledges, buddies, stakes) over public feeds. Couples already sleep in sync, and incumbents' own social sleep stories are about couples and families.

### Cited Findings
- Drews et al. (Frontiers in Psychiatry, 2020; 12 young couples, 4 nights of polysomnography): sleep architecture was more synchronised during co-sleep (coupled within about ±5 min), and co-sleep brought more REM sleep. Synchronisation was not affected by gender or chronotype. Synchronisation correlated with relationship depth — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/article/10.3389/fpsyt.2020.00583/full); [Frontiers news](https://www.frontiersin.org/news/2020/06/25/better-sleep-with-a-partner)
- Troxel and colleagues (SLEEP 2014 abstract, actigraphy, 46 couples): same-bed couples were awake or asleep at the same time about 75% of the time; wives' relationship satisfaction predicted sleep-wake concordance — [Sleep Review](https://sleepreviewmag.com/?p=16203) (via search summary, not verified)
- Oura's showcase Circles story is a married couple; WHOOP sells family memberships — [Oura blog](https://ouraring.com/blog/ar/how-kati-and-jon-use-oura-circles/); [WHOOP family membership](https://www.whoop.com/family-membership)
- SleepTown and Forest use group goals where the group's progress depends on each member keeping the schedule (Forest's "plant together" model; SleepTown circles) — [App Store SleepTown](https://apps.apple.com/sg/app/sleeptown/id1210251567?uo=2)
- BuddyClock (CSCW 2008): sharing sleep status with a small set of friends changed behaviour and made people feel more connected — [ACM via Unpaywall](https://unpaywall.org/10.1145%2F1460563.1460660) (via search summary)
- STEP UP collaboration arm (teams of 3, shared points depending on one random member's performance) raised steps about 637 a day during the intervention but did not persist — [PubMed 31498375](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31498375/)
- Commitment and stakes: demand for commitment in the student incentive trial; group commitment helped bedtime procrastination in a feasibility study — [NBER w30375](https://nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w30375/w30375.pdf); [medRxiv](https://medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.04.16.23288655v1.full-text)
- WHOOP Teams are often formed around sports teams and workplaces (community posts on padel and pickleball teams) — [WHOOP community](https://www.community.whoop.com/t/pickleball-players-join-our-whoop-team/15901)

### Inferences
Ranked by fit with the evidence (inference, not tested):
1. **Couples and bed partners**: biologically coupled sleep, shared bedtime, shared disruption (snoring, kids). Social object: "our night", shared wind-down, kind nudges. Low comparison risk if framed as a team.
2. **Close-friend groups of 3–8 (Circles-style)**: private, reactions only, focused on inputs (bedtime kept, phone down). Matches Somnometer and BuddyClock "connectedness" findings and avoids the broadcast concern.
3. **Accountability buddy or pledge with stakes**: one person sees whether you were in bed by your target time. Supported by incentive and commitment evidence. Monetary stakes add complexity.
4. **Synchronous "lights out" moment**: a group taps out together at a shared bedtime. Counteracts the "friends keep me up" effect. Untested.
5. **Households and new parents**: shared night shifts with a baby are a natural cooperative unit (who did the 3am feed). No evidence found; worth user research.
6. **Workplace teams**: WHOOP Teams model; sleep as part of wellness challenges. Risk of perceived surveillance by employers.
7. **Public status feed (sleepmaxxing flex)**: exists on TikTok and Instagram but is aspirational content, not a habit loop, and carries the highest orthosomnia risk.

### Gaps
- No studies on parents or households sharing night-time duties via an app.
- No evidence on synchronous "lights out" mechanics.
- No data on workplace sleep challenges' effectiveness or acceptance.

## 6. Sharing behaviour: do people share sleep scores (Oura/WHOOP screenshots), and why?

### Takeaway
People do share sleep scores publicly, but mainly as lifestyle and status content (celebrities, wellness influencers, sleepmaxxing) or as "hack" content on TikTok. It is not a high-frequency, peer-to-peer habit like posting a run. Oura added shareable stickers and Circles to support this.

### Cited Findings
- Celebrities (Jennifer Aniston, Gwyneth Paltrow, Kim Kardashian, Prince Harry) wear Oura. Aniston has posted screenshots of her Oura scores, sometimes in Instagram Story back-and-forths with Paltrow. Oura earned $10.7M in earned media value in 2021, up 282% (Tribe Dynamics) — [Glossy](https://www.glossy.co/fashion/how-the-oura-ring-became-the-celebrity-health-tracker-of-choice)
- Oura built shareable "Stickers" to post scores to social media, and Circles lets friends react with emoji (for example a crown for a great Sleep Score). The Oura ring is described as a status symbol, with some buying luxury covers up to $10,000 — [Oura support](https://support.ouraring.com/hc/en-us/articles/1500008536082); [ynetnews](https://www.ynetnews.com/health_science/article/hysw11ciwme)
- "Sleepmaxxing" is a catch-all term for any tip, gadget or hack for sleeping faster, longer and deeper. TikTok trends include mouth taping and the "sleepy girl mocktail" (tart cherry juice plus magnesium). The evidence for both is thin, and one cited tart-cherry study was industry-funded — [National Geographic](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/science/article/does-sleepmaxxing-work); [Tom's Guide](https://tomsguide.com/wellness/sleep/gp-reveals-which-tiktok-sleep-trends-are-actually-worth-trying)
- An AASM survey found more than a third of Americans use "sleep hacks" (including mouth taping and the sleepy girl mocktail); another survey found only 9% tried mouth taping — [Tom's Guide](https://tomsguide.com/wellness/sleep/is-tiktok-the-new-bedtime-story-new-survey-shows-americans-are-embracing-viral-sleep-hacks)
- Somnometer users valued sharing with friends for connectedness but disliked broadcasting — [Somnometer paper](https://www.eti.uni-siegen.de/ubicomp/papers/ess_ijhci2013.pdf)

### Inferences
- Public sharing of sleep scores looks like status signalling (owning a premium device, being "optimised") and content for followers, not a peer reciprocity loop. Kudos on a sleep score rewards a mostly involuntary outcome, so the social meaning is weaker than kudos on a run.
- What spreads on TikTok is routines and products (wind-down rituals, hacks), not individual nights. Shareable artefacts for a sleep app are more likely to be streaks, routines, and "bedtime kept" badges than raw scores.

### Gaps
- No quantitative data found on how often Oura or WHOOP users share scores (sticker use, share rates).
- No TikTok view counts for #sleepmaxxing, #mouthtaping or #sleepygirlmocktail were found; these would need TikTok Creative Center or a social analytics tool.
- No academic study found on motives for posting sleep data publicly.
